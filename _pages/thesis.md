## Thesis title

<strong>Energy Harvesting from Vortex Induced Vibration in MEMS Devices Using Magnetic Interaction</strong>

## Research overview

This thesis focuses on the design and analysis of a piezomagnetoelastic MEMS energy harvester operating under vortex-induced vibration (VIV). The central idea is to exploit the interaction between structural dynamics, magnetic effects, and ambient flow-induced oscillation to improve the amount of electrical energy harvested from small-scale mechanical motion.

The work is grounded in the understanding that conventional linear harvesters often struggle to operate efficiently when excitation is weak, broadband, or highly fluctuating. By introducing nonlinear and bistable dynamics, the harvester can respond more effectively to varying vibration conditions and potentially enhance power output over a broader range of operating states.

## Problem statement

Ambient flow environments such as air or water currents often produce periodic or quasi-periodic vibration that is not always strong enough for conventional harvesting structures. In MEMS-scale devices, this problem becomes even more pronounced because the system is highly sensitive to small perturbations, damping, and geometric configuration.

The thesis addresses this challenge by investigating how magnetic interaction can be used to influence the device response and improve energy conversion under vortex-induced vibration.

## Objectives

The research aimed to:

- model the nonlinear dynamics of a piezomagnetoelastic MEMS harvester;
- study the influence of vortex-induced excitation on system response;
- analyze the effect of magnetic interaction on bistable behavior and energy capture;
- interpret the coupling between structural nonlinearity and electrical power generation;
- identify design insights relevant to small-scale energy-autonomous systems.

## Methodology

The study combines mathematical modeling and numerical simulation with a focus on dynamic behavior. The methodology includes:

- development of a coupled electromechanical model for the MEMS energy harvester;
- time-domain analysis of vibration response under periodic and flow-driven excitation;
- phase-plane and nonlinear dynamic analysis to study bistability and transitions between stable states;
- evaluation of output response and harvested energy under different operating conditions;
- interpretation of how magnetic interaction modifies the effective stiffness and dynamic response.

## Key findings

The results suggest that nonlinear and magnetic effects can substantially improve the harvester's ability to extract energy from ambient vibration. In particular, the bistable or multistable configuration helps the structure interact with weak excitation more effectively, enabling repeated transitions between stable states that contribute to power generation.

The thesis highlights that the performance of the harvester depends sensitively on the balance between:

- excitation strength;
- magnetic coupling;
- structural stiffness and nonlinear restoring forces;
- electromechanical damping and energy extraction.

These findings reinforce the importance of design optimization in MEMS-scale energy harvesting, where small changes in configuration can have a strong effect on performance.

## Relevance to engineering practice

Although the thesis is rooted in fundamental nonlinear dynamics, it has strong practical relevance for low-power systems, distributed sensing, and self-powered electronic devices. The design insights are valuable for scenarios where ambient motion can be harvested reliably without relying on large external power sources.

This work also aligns with my broader engineering perspective: systems perform best when they are designed with both physics-based understanding and practical validation in mind.

- translating fundamental dynamics into useful engineering design insight.

These skills continue to shape my work in ADAS validation, control systems, estimation, and simulation-driven engineering today.
