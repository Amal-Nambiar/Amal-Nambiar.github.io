## Research motivation

Many practical environments contain low-level vibrational energy that is unnoticed by conventional systems. For MEMS-scale devices, this energy is often too weak or too irregular for linear energy harvesters to capture efficiently. The goal of the thesis was to investigate whether nonlinear mechanisms could help turn these small oscillations into a more usable and reliable source of power.

## Main concept

The device behavior was studied through the lens of:

- bistability and nonlinear response;
- piezomagnetoelastic coupling;
- vortex-induced excitation from ambient flow;
- electromechanical energy conversion;
- dynamic response tuning for enhanced power generation.

## Why magnetic interaction matters

Magnetic interaction introduces a controllable coupling mechanism that can reshape the effective stiffness and dynamic response of the harvester. This is especially important in MEMS devices, where even small adjustments in nonlinear force balance can have a strong influence on output performance.

In other words, magnetic interaction helps the structure move more effectively between stable states, allowing the system to respond to weak or varying environmental excitation in a more robust manner.

## Engineering insight

This research taught me a broader lesson that remains central to my engineering work today: systems that appear small or simple at first glance can still behave in highly nonlinear, rich, and physically meaningful ways. Understanding those dynamics is essential for designing robust devices and control strategies in real applications.

## Research takeaway

The central takeaway from this work is that nonlinear design can be a powerful route to improve energy harvesting performance in challenging environments. Rather than forcing a system to behave linearly, it is often more effective to understand and exploit its nonlinear dynamics for practical benefit.
