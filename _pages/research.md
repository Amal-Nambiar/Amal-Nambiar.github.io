## Research problem

Traditional linear energy harvesters are effective only near resonance. In real environments, excitation is often weak, broadband, or fluctuating, which reduces their effective power output. This is especially relevant for MEMS devices operating under ambient flow conditions.

My research addresses this by using nonlinear magnetic interaction to create bistable and broadband responses in piezomagnetoelastic energy harvesters. The idea is to move beyond narrowband resonance and produce a system that remains useful under varied, real-world operating conditions.

## Core themes

- nonlinear vibration and bistability in MEMS systems;
- vortex-induced vibration and fluid-structure interaction;
- piezomagnetoelastic coupling for enhanced energy conversion;
- broadband response under fluctuating excitation;
- phase-space, bifurcation, and chaos-based analysis;
- practical design for self-powered electronics and sensing systems.

## Scientific contribution

The research demonstrates that magnetic nonlinearity can significantly broaden the operating range of an energy harvester and improve energy capture compared with a purely linear design. It shows that carefully tuned bistability and chaotic dynamics can support more robust energy extraction under practical operating conditions.
